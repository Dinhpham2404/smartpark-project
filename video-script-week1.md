# Week 1 Stakeholder Update — Video Script

**Target length:** about 1 minute 40 seconds. Read at a normal pace.

---

Hi, I'm Tam Pham from $oftware ¢orp., and this is the week one update on the Smart Parking Platform.

This week we did three things.

**First, research.** We looked at what already exists in the market. Smart parking is not an empty space — it was about a four billion dollar market in 2024, and it's growing around twelve percent a year. The main players are SpotHero and ParkWhiz for garage reservations, ParkMobile and PayByPhone for meter payments, and companies like TIBA and Flowbird on the operator side.

The important finding is that no one covers both sides well. Driver apps and garage systems are usually separate products from separate vendors. That's why you see complaints about reservations not being honored at the gate — the app sold a space the garage didn't actually have.

**That leads to our differentiation.** We're building one platform for both drivers and operators, running on one shared live inventory. Availability comes from the garage's real occupancy feed, not from an estimated block of spaces. The price we show is the price we charge. And entry has a backup — a QR pass plus license plate recognition — so one failure doesn't strand the driver at the gate.

**Second, Vision and Scope.** We documented how the project was acquired, introduced the company, and defined what's in and out of the first release. In scope: garage reservations, payments, the driver map, and the operator dashboard. Out of scope for now: on-street parking, EV charging, and valet.

**Third, the SRS draft.** We delivered the first draft of the requirements specification with twenty-two use cases, covering drivers, operators, finance, and administrators, plus twelve non-functional requirements.

Next week we move into stakeholder analysis and expand the requirements with diagrams and acceptance criteria.

The full document is in the public GitHub repo, linked with this video. Thanks.
